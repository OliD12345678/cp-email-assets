Why Pilates Is for ALL Bodies

Every age. Every size. Every starting point. Here's why Pilates meets you exactly where you are, and what the research says about it.

There's a picture a lot of people have in their heads when they hear the word "Pilates." Someone young, flexible, already fit. If that picture has ever kept you (or a friend) from walking through our doors, this one's for you.

Club Pilates said it plainly in its own story on the subject: the idea that Pilates is only for women who already have a fitness routine is a misconception.1 Pilates is for athletes and grandparents, for people coming back from an injury and people who've never taken a fitness class in their lives. Here's why.

"No matter your age or body type, it's never too late to increase mobility and strength."

Jen Devine, Club Pilates instructor, in "Pilates for Every Body"

01It was built to help people heal

Pilates didn't start in a trendy studio. During World War I, Joseph Pilates was held at the Knockaloe internment camp on the Isle of Man, where he trained fellow internees in his method, which he called "Contrology." He later said he rigged springs to hospital beds there so people who couldn't stand could still exercise, an idea often credited as an early ancestor of the Reformer (historians note the bed-spring story comes from Pilates himself).2 He brought the method to New York City in 1926.2

In other words, from day one this was a method designed for bodies that needed something gentler, smarter, and more adaptable than "push harder."

02The springs adjust to you, not the other way around

The Reformer uses springs instead of fixed weights, so the same exercise can be made lighter, heavier, more supported, or more challenging just by changing the setup. Harvard Health describes Pilates as a good low-impact option, especially as we get older, because it builds core strength, balance, and flexibility and can be adapted on a mat, a Reformer, or even a chair to fit different abilities and comfort levels.3

That's why two people of completely different ages and fitness levels can take the same class, side by side, and both get exactly the workout they need.

03There's a level for every starting point

At Club Pilates you don't have to "be in shape" to begin. Classes are organized into levels so you can build at your own pace:4

04Your instructor is trained to modify

Club Pilates instructors go through a 450-hour comprehensive training program that covers all eight pieces of Pilates apparatus, and includes a dedicated "special populations" component: how to modify for injury, age, and fitness level.5 As the Club Pilates story puts it, instructors work with clients to provide modifications for any ailments they experience.1

Tight hips, a cranky knee, a stiff back, a body that's just getting started? Tell your instructor. Adjusting for you is part of the job, not a special request.

05The science backs it up

This isn't just a feel-good message. Research has looked at Pilates for exactly the people who often assume it isn't for them:

People with back pain

A Cochrane review of randomized trials found Pilates reduced pain and disability compared with minimal intervention for people with low back pain.6

Older adults

Meta-analyses have found Pilates improves balance in older adults, with promising results for strength and lowering fall risk.78

People with achy joints

In the Club Pilates story, an instructor's mother was steered toward Pilates by her physician because it wouldn't add stress to her arthritic joints.1

Moms-to-be

Guidance from the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists lists modified Pilates among activities considered safe during pregnancy, with your provider's OK.9

06It's about how you feel, not how you look

Pilates measures progress in things you can feel: getting up off the floor more easily, standing taller, carrying groceries without a twinge, sleeping better after a good class. Those wins don't belong to one body type. They belong to anyone who shows up.

Our studios are full of proof. Members in their teens and members in their eighties. Former athletes and first-timers. People rebuilding after surgery and people training for their next race. Same Reformers, same community, each person working at their own level.
