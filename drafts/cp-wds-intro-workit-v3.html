ONE

 

You don't need to be flexible.
That's rather the point.

 

Almost everyone tells us this on the way in, usually with a small apologetic laugh. Flexibility isn't what gets you in the door. It's what you leave with.

 

In a 2024 study, thirty-two women classified as sedentary did eight weeks of Pilates and gained an average of 7.8 centimetres on a sit-and-reach test, about three inches. Roughly the difference between reaching your shins and reaching past your toes. A separate eight-week trial found the change measurable by week six.

 

TWO

 

Better balance, and quietly,
less worry about falling

 

In 2021, researchers pooled twelve randomized trials covering 702 healthy older adults to see what Pilates actually did for balance. Functional reach, how far you can lean before you have to step, improved by more than nine centimetres.

 
  The clearest result in the whole review wasn't a physical measurement at all. It was how much less afraid people were of falling.
 

That's the thing our members mention without being asked. Not a number on a scale. Steady coming down the stairs with a laundry basket. Getting off the ground after playing with a grandchild without having to think about how.

 

THREE

 

It is real strength work,
without the pounding

 

People assume Pilates is a gentler cousin of stretching. It is gentler on your joints, certainly. That isn't the same as easy.

 

In that same 2024 study, over eight weeks the women added four to five kilos to their leg strength test, around ten pounds, without a single barbell or minute of impact. The reformer's springs support you as much as they challenge you, which is precisely why it works for people who can't, or would rather not, do high-impact exercise.

 
 
 

The hardest part is the first thirty minutes

 

If you've read this far, the research probably isn't what's holding you back. It's usually the walking-in part: a room full of equipment you've never used, and people who seem to already know what they're doing.

 

Which is exactly why the free Intro Class exists. Thirty minutes, one instructor, and a proper introduction to the reformer: the carriage, the springs, the straps, and the handful of movements you'll actually be asked to do.
